LoRA effect

Train a LoRA video effect

Video LoRA is a lightweight 'plugin' method for enhancing a pre-trained image- or video-generation model.

This 'plugin' is very small and only requires training a tiny subset of the model's parameters rather than retraining the entire large model.

The training process is like installing a small add-on that teaches the model new motions or styles—such as learning a dance routine.

Once trained, a Video LoRA captures a specific motion or style and can be applied repeatedly to any compatible photo to reproduce the same effect.

Get started

1

Decide What Effect You Want

  • Pick a clear motion (dance, wave, spin)
  • Ask: "What should the video do?"
  • Write a one-sentence description
2

Understand What a Dataset Is

  • A dataset is a collection of example videos/frames
  • Think of them as "practice clips"
  • Each video teaches the LoRA plugin
3

Find or Create Your Video Dataset

  • Search online for Creative Commons or royalty-free clips (Pixabay, Pexels, YouTube)
  • Look for clips that match your effect
  • Aim for 50-200 short clips (5-10 seconds)
  • Record videos if needed
4

Prepare Your Dataset

  • Download/collect clips into one folder
  • Use a video-to-frames tool (2-5 fps)
  • Check for clear frames (good lighting, no blurry motion)
5

Train Your Video LoRA

  • Open a LoRA-supporting tool
  • Load the base model
  • Choose "Fine-tune with LoRA"
  • Point to the frames folder
  • Name the template
  • Hit "Train"
6

Test and Apply

  • Load the new LoRA template after training
  • Input a new photo/clip
  • Render to see the effect applied

Tips

  • Start with a narrow effect (one dance move, not entire routines)
  • Quality over quantity—better to have 50 clear clips than 200 blurry ones
  • Keep your dataset organized and well-labeled
